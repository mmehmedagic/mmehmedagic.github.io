MS Thesis

  • The Zig-Zag Pseudorandom Generator

    Abstract: Two seminal works proved that the problem of undirected s - t connectivity is decidable deterministically in logarithmic space. Reingold originally proved this fact while Rozenman and Vadhan gave an alternative proof. These two proofs remain the only ones demonstrating this fact. Rozenman and Vadhan use the iterated application of the derandomized square in their proof. They also show that this product could be used to analyze the Impagliazzo-Nisan-Wigderson (INW) pseudorandom generator. Even before Rozenman and Vadhan's work, the INW generator had been used to fool various models of computation, but many newer proofs that the INW generator fools other models of computation often hinge on analysis through the derandomized square product. For instance, it fools unbounded-width permutation branching programs with a single accept state, with nearly optimal seed length. Reingold's algorithm relies on the iterated application of a different graph product called the zig-zag product, previously introduced in a work by Reingold, Vadhan, and Wigderson. The zig-zag product was later shown to similarly have pseudorandom walk properties on regular graphs. In this thesis, we describe the construction of a "zig-zag pseudorandom generator." Prior work had already described a pseudorandom walk generator based on the zig-zag product, which was used to visit all vertices in a connected undirected graph. Our contribution is that this "zig-zag pseudorandom generator" can also (like the INW generator) be used to generate pseudorandom bits that fool permutation branching programs with a single accept state, with a seed length of O ( log  n ⋅ ( log  log  n + log ( 1 / ε ) ) ) . The generator is constructed without reference to the zig-zag product though it mimics the iterative construction in Reingold's algorithm under the hood. Our analysis will make use of the zig-zag product's spectral expansion properties and recently developed singular-value approximation techniques of random walk matrices.

    Mentor: William Hoza

REU Papers

  • Markov Chain Mixing and Random Walks on Regular Graphs

    Abstract: Markov chains are stochastic processes in which the next state depends only on the current state. As such, they are able to readily model various practical processes. One such process is the simple random walk on graphs, which are themselves reductions of systems like networks, molecules, etc. Since mixing involves the rate of convergence of a Markov chain to a so- called “stationary distribution,” we aim to understand mixing for this simple random walk, specifically on the class of regular graphs, while developing the theory of Markov chain mixing with other examples along the way.

    Mentor: Stephen Yearwood

  • Geometric Measure Theory with Methods from Algorithmic Complexity

    Abstract: Since the development of Hausdorff dimension, there have been many attempts both to calculate the dimension of specific fractals and to find generalized descriptions of dimension by set transformations, classifications, etc. These calculation techniques and theorems have formed the basis for the field of geometric measure theory. More recently, the theory of computation has provided an alternative way to view this field: through the use of the point-to-set principle, mathematicians have been able to use the Kolmogorov complexity of binary strings to further generalize the fascinating theorems of the field.

    Mentor: Donald Stull
